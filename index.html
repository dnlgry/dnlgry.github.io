How can counselling help?

Life can be complicated. Sometimes we find ourselves struggling with feelings, relationships or patterns we don't fully understand. Whether you're navigating anxiety, low mood, addiction, questions of identity or a difficult period of change or loss, you don't have to work through it alone.

Counselling provides a space to explore the challenges you're facing, and also the patterns, relationships and experiences that often lie beneath them. It isn't about imposing answers or telling you how you should live your life. Instead, we'll work together using curiosity, thoughtful reflection and, where helpful, gentle challenge to support you in finding your own way forward.

The counselling process

Everyone comes to counselling with their own experiences and hopes for change. While there's no fixed path, it can help to know what working together might look like. I generally suggest starting with six weekly sessions, giving us time to get to know each other and begin exploring what matters to you. We can then adjust the frequency and duration of our work together as your needs evolve.

How the process might unfold…

01 An exploratory conversation

We begin with a free, 30-minute online conversation. This is an opportunity to tell me a little about what's bringing you to counselling, ask any questions and get a feel for how I work. It's also a chance for us to explore what you're looking for and whether working together feels like a good fit. There's no obligation to continue.

02 Getting to know you

Our first sessions are an opportunity to begin understanding you and your world. We'll explore your experiences, relationships and circumstances, what might be troubling you, and what you hope to gain from counselling. We might also consider how earlier experiences have shaped the way you understand yourself and relate to others.

There's no expectation that you'll know exactly what to say or where to begin. Getting to know someone takes time, and this understanding will continue to deepen throughout our work.

03 Finding our direction

As we develop a clearer understanding of what brings you to counselling, we'll begin to consider what you'd like to work towards. This might involve navigating a particular difficulty, exploring recurring patterns, making sense of past experiences or engaging with broader questions about identity, relationships and meaning.

Together, we'll develop a flexible plan that reflects your needs, goals and preferences. It's not a rigid programme or a predetermined destination. Our direction can evolve as new understandings emerge and your circumstances change.

04 The work

Much of the therapeutic work happens through conversation, reflection and the relationship we develop. We'll explore your thoughts, feelings, experiences and relationships, paying attention to patterns and possibilities that may not always be immediately apparent.

Depending on what feels useful, our work might involve making sense of difficult emotions, gently challenging familiar ways of thinking, exploring unconscious influences, developing practical ways of responding to challenges, or finding new perspectives on yourself and your life.

Some sessions may feel illuminating, others challenging, and some simply offer a much-needed space to pause and be heard. Meaningful change rarely follows a straight line. We'll work at a pace that respects your circumstances, readiness and autonomy.

05 Reviewing and reflecting

Throughout our work, we'll take time to reflect on how things are going, what's changing, what remains difficult and whether counselling is meeting your needs. Your feedback is an important part of this process, and we'll adjust our approach or direction whenever it feels appropriate.

Counselling might be brief and focused or develop into something longer and more exploratory. When the time comes to finish, we'll make space to reflect on what we've discovered together, acknowledge the work you've undertaken and consider what you might carry forward.

A bit about me

Prior to undertaking integrative psychotherapy training in the UK and completing postgraduate counselling study in Australia, my career spanned research, public policy, consulting, and thought leadership, underpinned by studies in physics, law, and public policy and management.

My interests span psychology, philosophy, science, music, and art, united by a fascination with the human condition. I also remain committed to personal growth and professional development through regular personal therapy, clinical supervision, study, training, and being involved with communities of practice.

As a result, I bring warmth, therapeutic skill and broad life experience to my practice, alongside a genuine curiosity about people and the complexity of their inner worlds.

My therapeutic approach

My approach is relational, integrative and depth-oriented, attentive not only to what's happening in your life, but also to the patterns, experiences and unconscious influences that may shape it. I'm particularly interested in the unconscious, the search for meaning, and the complex interplay between our inner lives, our relationships and the world around us.

I won't presume to have the answers or tell you how to live. Instead, I'll work alongside you to explore your experiences, gently challenge familiar ways of thinking and open up new possibilities for understanding yourself and relating to others.

I believe meaningful change grows through a trusting therapeutic relationship, where we can approach difficult experiences with honesty, curiosity and compassion. Whether our work is brief and focused or longer and more exploratory, the relationship we build will be at its heart.
